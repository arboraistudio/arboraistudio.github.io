The Daily Reality

If several of these sound familiar, the workflow below is usually the right first pilot.

Quote requests arrive by email, forms, WhatsApp, and spreadsheets with missing shipment details.

Teams copy shipper, consignee, route, cargo, date, and reference information between systems.

Customers repeatedly request status updates that staff assemble from separate operational sources.

Shipment documents are checked and renamed manually before the next team can use them.

Exceptions are discussed in inboxes and chats without a consistent owner or escalation path.

Customer updates need review when a shipment is delayed, incomplete, or outside normal rules.

How the Workflow Runs

Every item of work passes through the same reviewed pipeline, from capture to confirmation.

01

Capture

Receive the quote request, shipment document, status question, or exception from an approved channel.

02

Extract

Structure route, cargo, dates, references, parties, document type, and requested action.

03

Validate

Check required fields and rules, then flag missing details or unusual cases.

04

Prepare

Draft the internal task, quote input, status response, document record, or escalation.

05

Review

Operations staff approve customer-facing updates and sensitive system actions.

06

Update

Write the approved outcome to the CRM, transport system, tracker, document store, or report.

The Pilot Stays Contained

  • One high-friction workflow
  • One primary intake channel
  • One review and approval path
  • One destination system or operational tracker
  • Exception handling, documentation, and handover included

Documentation, training, and handover are part of every pilot.

What This Is Not

  • Not a replacement for your CRM, ERP, accounting platform, or core business system
  • No sensitive customer or operational action without the agreed review step
  • No broad transformation programme before one contained workflow proves useful
  • No autonomous carrier selection, customs, compliance, rate, or dispatch decisions

A Good Fit When

We would rather tell you early if this does not fit. It usually does when:

  • Your team repeatedly handles quote requests, shipment documents, or status questions.
  • Information moves between email, messaging, spreadsheets, CRM, and transport systems by copy-paste.
  • Exceptions need clear ownership and a human decision.
  • You can isolate one workflow and one destination system for the first pilot.
